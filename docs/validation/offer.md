# Digital Estate Assessment offer

## One-sentence offer

Contexla Digital Estate Assessment gives organizations an evidence-backed view
of what exists in their CMS estate, what it depends on, and what creates cost,
risk, or migration complexity before they commit to modernization work.

## Buyer problem

Organizations often plan a CMS upgrade, replatform, consolidation, or cost-
reduction program without a reliable inventory of sites, modules, content
models, components, integrations, infrastructure, and custom functionality.
The unknowns lead to weak estimates, missed dependencies, and avoidable project
risk.

## Initial ideal customer profile

An organization is a strong candidate when it has:

- one or more Sitecore or Drupal estates;
- an upcoming renewal, upgrade, migration, consolidation, procurement, or audit;
- incomplete or outdated architecture documentation;
- multiple stakeholders who disagree about what is in use;
- concern about integrations, custom code, accessibility, operational ownership,
  or migration complexity;
- a decision that must be made within roughly the next two quarters.

Likely sponsors include a CIO/CTO, digital or web director, enterprise architect,
application owner, or modernization program lead. Procurement and security are
important participants but are unlikely to be the primary problem owner.

## Trigger events

- Sitecore license renewal or exit evaluation;
- Drupal major-version planning or inherited Drupal estate review;
- redesign or replatform procurement;
- merger, consolidation, or new ownership of a digital estate;
- accessibility or governance concern;
- vendor transition or loss of institutional knowledge;
- unreliable migration estimates;
- pressure to reduce hosting, licensing, or maintenance cost.

## Deliverable

The bounded assessment produces:

- an executive summary tied to the customer's decision;
- an evidence-backed estate inventory;
- system, site, module/package, content-model, component, integration, and
  infrastructure views within the agreed scope;
- risks, unknowns, and evidence gaps;
- complexity and disposition recommendations such as retain, retire, replace,
  consolidate, investigate, or migrate;
- prioritized next actions;
- machine-readable inventory and findings where feasible.

It does not promise a complete migration plan, code-level security audit,
accessibility certification, or automatic discovery of systems the customer has
not made available.

## Engagement shape

The initial product is a fixed-scope professional assessment supported by
Contexla tooling. It should be sellable and deliverable even while some analysis
is manual.

Scope and price should be based on estate boundaries and decision value, not the
number of hours the future platform might save. A pilot may cover one Drupal
installation or a bounded slice of a Sitecore estate. Multi-estate discovery and
deep source-code assessment are follow-on scopes.

## Buyer-facing message

> Before choosing a migration target or approving a modernization budget, get a
> defensible inventory of the estate you actually operate. We collect approved
> evidence, identify dependencies and complexity, and show which areas need
> retirement, replacement, migration, or deeper investigation.

Avoid leading with OTP, Oban, Python isolation, or agent-generated Tools.
Those explain how Contexla can become efficient and adaptable; they are not the
outcome the initial buyer purchases.

## Delivery and expansion path

Offer the least-friction approved delivery mode:

- customer-run collection with an approved snapshot processed in an agreed
  secure environment;
- customer-hosted Contexla when evidence residency or network policy requires
  it;
- ongoing deployment only when repeated monitoring, verification, or assurance
  has a funded purpose.

A successful assessment may lead to modernization planning, remediation,
migration implementation, migration verification, or managed assurance. These
are legitimate consulting and recurring-revenue paths, but the assessment must
not predetermine that the agency receives the follow-on work.

Customer-specific analysis should be implemented through configuration or
bounded versioned extensions rather than a fork of the platform or Application.
