# Buyer discovery interview

Use this as a conversation guide, not a questionnaire to email. Ask about the
last concrete event and current decision before describing Contexla.

## Opening

> I am researching how organizations assess CMS estates before modernization,
> migration, or audit work. I would like to understand how you handle it today,
> where uncertainty remains, and what evidence decision-makers need. I am not
> asking you to evaluate a product presentation.

## Situation and trigger

1. What change or decision caused you to look at the estate now?
2. What CMSs, sites, teams, and environments might be in scope?
3. When must the organization make a decision?
4. Who owns the decision, and who can block it?
5. What happens if nothing changes this year?

## Current process

6. Tell me about the last time you inventoried or assessed this estate.
7. Who performed the work, how long did it take, and what did it cost?
8. What sources did they use: interviews, spreadsheets, APIs, databases,
   configuration exports, code, monitoring, or vendor documentation?
9. Which parts were most manual or unreliable?
10. What was discovered too late?

## Unknowns and consequences

11. What do you not trust about the current inventory or documentation?
12. Which integrations or custom functions are hardest to understand?
13. Which unknowns can change budget, schedule, target platform, or procurement?
14. Have missing dependencies caused an outage, delay, change order, or failed
    migration?
15. How do you currently distinguish what is configured from what is actually
    used?

## Evidence and access

16. What read-only evidence could you provide to an assessment team?
17. Could you run an approved collector and return a snapshot if direct access
    is prohibited?
18. Are source code, database metadata, configuration exports, and infrastructure
    diagrams available?
19. What security, privacy, procurement, or data-residency constraints apply?
20. Would analysis need to run entirely inside your environment?

## Desired output

21. What must the assessment tell you to make the pending decision?
22. What format will executives, architects, procurement, and delivery teams
    actually use?
23. Which findings need source evidence to be credible?
24. What would make an assessment actionable rather than another inventory
    spreadsheet?
25. How would you judge whether the assessment was worth paying for?

## Commercial qualification

26. Is there an approved initiative or budget behind this work?
27. Who could sponsor a bounded assessment?
28. What is the purchasing path and expected lead time?
29. Would a fixed-scope pilot for one installation or estate slice be useful?
30. What must be true for you to start within the next 60 days?

## Concept test after discovery

Only after understanding the situation, summarize the offer in two or three
sentences. Then ask:

- Which part is valuable for the decision you described?
- Which part is unnecessary?
- What is missing?
- What evidence would you refuse or be unable to provide?
- What would prevent you from buying a bounded pilot?

Do not ask whether the idea is “interesting.” Ask for a concrete next step: an
introduction to the sponsor, a technical evidence review, or permission to draft
a paid pilot scope.
