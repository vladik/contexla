# Vision and positioning

## Vision

Contexla is an extensible digital-content integration and automation platform.
It hosts independently useful Applications for integration, assessment,
validation, migration, monitoring, governance, transport, and archival work.

The product is both a commercial platform and a growing portfolio of solutions.
It can support consulting engagements, but it must not exist only as an internal
agency framework.

## Why now

Three changes create an opportunity:

1. Organizations have fragmented content estates and pressure to reduce the
   cost of legacy CMS platforms.
2. Accessibility, governance, provenance, and AI-generated content increase the
   need for validation and evidence across systems.
3. Agents make software more adaptable: users can express intent instead of
   manually configuring every screen, and a small team can generate bounded
   extensions faster.

Cheap code does not remove the need for a platform. It increases the importance
of durable execution, permissions, contracts, evidence, versioning, and safe
boundaries around generated code. Those are Contexla's foundation.

## Positioning

Contexla is not:

- a CMS or an AI-first CMS;
- a replacement for Drupal, Sitecore, Contentful, or similar products;
- a general-purpose automation platform for every business process;
- an autonomous agent whose output must be trusted without evidence;
- a single migration product disguised as a platform.

Contexla operates across content systems. It combines reusable integration and
execution infrastructure with domain-focused Applications that answer concrete
business questions.

## Five question families

The long-term portfolio answers five families of questions:

- **What exists?** Inventory, estate assessment, usage, dependencies, and
  technical debt.
- **What changed?** History, drift, regression, freshness, and explanation.
- **Is it correct?** Integrity, completeness, accessibility, policy, and source
  of truth validation.
- **Can it move forward?** Publishing gates, migration readiness, cutover, and
  approval.
- **What should happen next?** Routing, remediation, transformation, transport,
  and investigation.

## Commercial model hypothesis

The likely initial motion is consulting-led productization:

1. use an Application to sell and deliver a clearly scoped assessment or
   automation engagement;
2. turn repeated delivery work into reusable product functionality;
3. offer on-premise software where customer control, privacy, or network access
   matters;
4. develop repeatable licenses or subscriptions only after buyers demonstrate
   willingness to pay.

This is a hypothesis to validate. Product scope must be driven by paid needs,
not feature volume.
